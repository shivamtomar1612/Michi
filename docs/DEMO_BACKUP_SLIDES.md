# MICHI Offline Backup Slides — Phase 17

These text slides are presentation material, not claims of a completed pilot. Pair them with a fresh, dated capture of the actual public interface when needed.

## Slide 1 — Travel deeper. Leave lighter.

**Problem:** Discovery systems often amplify popular places without showing what is known about destination readiness or community terms.

**MICHI:** A responsible Japan travel and cultural-exchange platform designed to consider traveler fit, cultural context, local benefit, accessibility, availability, and destination health.

**Speaker note:** MICHI’s intended differentiation is the evidence and decision model, not a generic AI-generated itinerary.

## Slide 2 — Start with sources

- 3 catalog destinations: Kyoto, Kanazawa, Takayama.
- 15 place records.
- 3 source-backed external cultural listings in Kanazawa.
- The reviewed catalog snapshot is dated 2026-10-07.
- External listings remain operator-directed information; no MICHI booking is enabled.

**Speaker note:** The hosted catalog is not a partnership directory. The records link to their source and do not imply endorsement.

## Slide 3 — Responsible recommendations need evidence

**Inputs in the product design:** personal match, cultural depth, local benefit, accessibility, availability, and destination health.

**Current evidence boundary:** no MICHI hosts, no slots, no bookings, no current health signals, and no verified map coordinates in the hosted snapshot.

**Speaker note:** Do not show a percentage score or claim a quieter alternative unless the live service produces it from current verified evidence.

## Slide 4 — Community participation is controlled

- Hosts should control rules, visibility, and capacity.
- Confirmed bookings must remain valid if recommendations pause.
- DMO information is designed to be aggregated and non-personal.
- No MICHI host/operator is currently onboarded in the audited project.

**Speaker note:** A public tourism listing alone does not prove operator authorization or a MICHI relationship.

## Slide 5 — Measure before claiming impact

**Observed product instrumentation:** 8 destination-view, 9 health-view, and 28 Companion-question events. These sum to 45 event rows and do not count unique people.

**Not yet measured:** destination dispersion, local income, community sentiment, cultural learning, or reduced overtourism.

**Next step:** an 8–12 week, consent-led pilot with real participating operators and a pre-agreed measurement plan.

**Closing:** “MICHI should grow only where communities want to participate—and only claim the outcomes its evidence can support.”
